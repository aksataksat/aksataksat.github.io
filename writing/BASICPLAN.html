Basic Plan

An operational checklist for the 90-day reinvention framework. Proceed sequentially: diagnose first, design second, then execute.

Phase 1: The Reality Audit (Baseline Diagnosis)

Before setting a single goal or designing a routine, you must establish an objective baseline. Correction requires a precise current state to compare against a desired state (control theory). Do not attempt to fix anything during this phase—pure observation only.

1.2 Document the Evidence & Unpack States vs. Behaviors

  • Example: Instead of "bad finances", write: "Income: $2,500/mo, Debt: $8,000, Savings: $0, Weekly hours spent on income skills: 0".
  • • States (Outcomes you cannot directly perform today): Weight, bank balance, net worth, closeness with partner, books finished.
    • Behaviors (Direct, repeatable actions): What you eat, daily steps, lifting sessions, outreach sent, time spent reading, hours spent scrolling.

Phase 5: Reverse Engineering (The ABTH Framework)

Shrink the 90-day horizon down into a single day's concrete, repeatable assignment.

5.1 Run the ABTH Math

Gap = Becoming (Target) - Actual (Current)

  • Example: Target: 10 books (2,000 pages). 2,000 ÷ 90 days ≈ 22 pages/day.

5.2 Build Checkpoints & Funnel Pipelines

  • Example: $30,000 target → 10 deals → 30 sales calls → 150 qualified leads.

5.3 Schedule & Protect the Plan

  • "If [Cue: It is 7:00 AM / I finish dinner], then [Action: I immediately go for a walk / open my book]."

5.4 Build in Margins of Safety (Hedging)

  • • Volume Hedging: Aim for 30 pages/day instead of 22; schedule 150 outreach messages instead of 100.
    • Time Hedging: Build a timeline aimed at wrapping up work on Day 75 rather than Day 90.
    • Capacity Hedging: Leave deliberate white space on your weekly calendar instead of scheduling at 100% capacity.
    • Assumption Hedging: Build financial projections using conservative conversion rates (e.g., 20% close rate instead of 30%).

Phase 7: The Feedback Loop & Weekly Review

Treat the 90 days as a running scientific experiment: take action, gather objective data, isolate bottlenecks, and adjust your approach.

7.2 The Weekly Review Protocol

Set a recurring, weekly review slot and follow this 3-step sequence:

  • • Wins: Document what went well and what you executed consistently.
    • Lessons: Note patterns you learned about your capacity, energy, or environment.
    • Improvements: Define the single operational adjustment needed for the upcoming week.

7.3 Guidelines for Adjusting Your Plan

  • • Month 1 (Calibration): Focus on stabilizing your routine and discovering your true baseline capacity.
    • Month 2 (Optimization): Focus on resolving friction points, fixing bottlenecks, and building pacing efficiency.
    • Month 3 (Protection & Finishing): Protect against late-stage complacency and finish the remaining 20–30% of your plan with full focus.
