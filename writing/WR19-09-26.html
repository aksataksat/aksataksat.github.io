Impulse Buys and Similar

After experiencing the traps of consumerism, I have taken it upon myself to correct my wrongs and come up with a way to declutter my life, and save money for things that truly matter. 

From now on, all products that you want to buy shall be put in the Purchase simmer, a place where items one wants to buy are let to rest for some time (a page on a notebook basically) so you can clearly think about if you need them, this ensures that all impulse buys are cured. 

But there are situations, like the one I faced at a store where you do not get time to let a purchase simmer, or sometimes you are confused about the purchase even after letting it simmer, these two are very common and important cases, so for these we evaluate if a purchase is good by asking the following questions, and without any dishonesty to oneself, answer them in a voice to yourself:

-How much does this item cost in Rupees?
-What percentage are those Rupees of my current spendable funds?
-Will this product make me happy?
-If so, how will it make me happy?
-Will it keep making me happy 1 week after   the purchase?
-Do I have space to keep this?
-Where will I keep it?
-Will it add clutter to that location? If so, is it   worth the headache?
-Is this good quality, or should I collect funds   for a better quality one?
-Is this priced correctly, will waiting for a sale   help?
-Will I look back on this purchase many   months later and think that the purchase   was worth it, or the money would have   better been   kept for something else?
-Is this a blatant Luxury?
-Am I buying this for my own happiness or to   show something to others?
-Am I buying this as an escape from some   other problem?
-Do I actually deserve this right now, or   should I be focusing on something else?

Yes, they are many, but answering all honestly takes 5 minutes, and can save much more time, effort and money than that. Use this on all, even simmered products benefit from these, and for any on the spot purchase, do not dare miss these. 

Sorry to state the obvious but these do not apply to bare essentials like basic household, food, water or purchases made under certain situational and/or social commitments. 

These questions were inspired by my father.
